xAI Colossus, Memphis

xAI built the Colossus AI supercluster in Memphis, Tennessee — 100,000 GPUs stood up in 122 days, doubled to 200,000 within three more months, with Dell Technologies and Supermicro supplying the GPU servers. Each server carries eight GPUs; racks are liquid-cooled at 64 GPUs per rack; every GPU gets a dedicated 400 GbE network interface on an NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet fabric. The expansion toward one million GPUs runs on Dell-built NVIDIA Blackwell (B200/GB200) systems. This drawing renders that setup out of the digital twins in this repository.

xAI (Elon Musk's AI company) built one of the world's largest AI computers, called Colossus, in Memphis, Tennessee. It is made of racks — tall cabinets — of GPU servers (computers built around graphics processors, the chips AI training runs on), many of them supplied by Dell. The racks are cooled with liquid instead of air because the chips run too hot for fans alone, and every GPU has its own very fast network connection so 100,000+ of them can work on one problem together. What the twins would show you

Each link opens a twin paused at the step that carries this setup's story (the twin must be running; the trace loads at that step).

Each link opens one of the interactive models paused at the exact moment that matters for this story — like opening a video at a bookmarked scene. The model has to be running first (see the chips above).

  • XE9680 at the fuse — eight GPUs snap into one NVLink domain on the baseboard, atomically, and the counter never grows past 8: the domain stops at the chassis wall. Colossus's first build did this ~12,500 times.
  • XE9680 joining the fabric — eight NICs train, one per GPU (~3.6 Tb/s per server): the step where a box becomes a cluster member.
  • XE9712 at the fuse — the step where 72 GPUs snap into a single NVLink domain, all at once or not at all. Colossus's expansion racks do this 8,000+ times over.
  • XE9712 waiting on coolant — "liquid before silicon": the rack pauses until the loop proves itself.
  • SN6000 under congestion — the busiest link driven past 95% with zero packets dropped; Colossus's training traffic lives here.
  • IR7000 at leak/flow verify — the longest stage of the cooling trace, and the thing the XE9712's coolant step is waiting for.
  • Exascale at full fan-out — all four data servers streaming at once; the read pattern that becomes the fabric's incast.
What the sources actually state: Dell and Supermicro supplied the servers; 100k GPUs in 122 days, then 200k; 8-GPU HGX servers, 64 GPUs per liquid-cooled rack, ~1,500 racks; 400 GbE per GPU on Spectrum-X; a ~$5B Dell order of Blackwell systems for the expansion. The specific rack counts drawn here, the four-rack grouping, and the storage tier are illustrative — xAI has not published its storage architecture, so the Exascale twin is a representative stand-in, not a claim. What the news reports actually say: Dell and Supermicro built the servers; 100,000 GPUs were running within 122 days and 200,000 soon after; each server holds 8 GPUs, each rack 64, roughly 1,500 racks; every GPU has its own fast network port. Everything else in the drawing — how many racks are shown, how the storage works — is our illustration, not something xAI has confirmed. Blocks tagged "representative" are educated stand-ins.
