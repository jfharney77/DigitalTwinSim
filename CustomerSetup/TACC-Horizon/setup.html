TACC Horizon, Austin

The Texas Advanced Computing Center's Horizon is the largest academic supercomputer in the United States — 300 petaflops, ten times TACC's Frontera. Announced November 2025, it is built from Dell Integrated Rack Scalable Systems (IRSS): direct-liquid-cooled Dell PowerEdge servers on the NVIDIA Grace Blackwell platform, one million CPU cores and 4,000 NVIDIA GPUs, joined by NVIDIA Quantum-X800 InfiniBand. This is the rare public deployment that maps almost one-to-one onto this repo's AI Factory quartet.

The Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC) at UT Austin runs the biggest university supercomputer in the US, called Horizon. Dell delivers it as complete pre-built racks — cabinets of servers that arrive assembled, tested, and plumbed for liquid cooling (the chips run too hot for air alone). Inside are a million processor cores and 4,000 NVIDIA GPUs, wired together by an ultra-fast network called InfiniBand so they can behave like one machine. Almost every part of this setup has a matching interactive model in this repository.

What the twins would show you

Each link opens a twin paused at the step that carries this setup's story (the twin must be running; the trace loads at that step).

Each link opens one of the interactive models paused at the exact moment that matters for this story. The model has to be running first (see the chips above).

  • iDRAC at ready — the BMC watching over a host that hasn't powered on yet; multiply by every tray in Horizon.
  • IR7000 at leak/flow verify — the longest stage of the cooling trace, and the gate the racks wait behind.
  • XE9712 waiting on coolant — "liquid before silicon", the inversion no air-cooled twin has.
  • XE9712 at the fuse — GPUs-in-domain snapping from 0 to 72; Horizon does this once per rack.
  • QuantumX800 computing routes — the longest fabric stage: one subnet manager computes every forwarding table before a byte moves, then leaves the data path.
  • QuantumX800 at the SHARP crossing — fabric traffic falls while the effective all-reduce rate rises: the switches are doing the arithmetic.
  • QuantumX800 under the incast burst — senders wait microseconds for credits instead of losing anything; the sent-without-credit counter cannot move.
What the sources actually state: Horizon is built by Dell for TACC; Dell IRSS with direct-liquid-cooled PowerEdge servers; NVIDIA Grace Blackwell platform with Vera CPUs; 1M CPU cores, 4,000 GPUs; Quantum-X800 InfiniBand; 300 PF, 10× Frontera. The three-rack row, per-rack internals, the ~56-rack estimate (4,000 GPUs ÷ 72 per NVL72-class rack), and the management plane are drawn from the twins' stylized models, not from a published TACC floor plan. What Dell and TACC have actually said: Dell builds Horizon as pre-integrated, liquid-cooled racks; it uses NVIDIA's Grace Blackwell chips; a million CPU cores and 4,000 GPUs; InfiniBand networking; 300 petaflops. The way we've drawn the row — three racks, what's inside each, the management layer — is our stylized illustration based on how these systems are normally built, not TACC's actual floor plan.
